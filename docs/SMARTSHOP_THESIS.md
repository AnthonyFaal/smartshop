# SmartShop: the thesis

## One sentence

SmartShop wins by becoming the trusted operating layer for fast, local commerce around UMaT—not by becoming a smaller copy of a national online marketplace.

## The customer problem

Students and nearby residents already discover goods through WhatsApp, Instagram, friends and physical shops. Those channels are excellent at discovery but weak at the moment money changes hands: stock is uncertain, delivery is improvised, identity is unclear, disputes are personal, and repeat buying has no memory.

Small sellers have the complementary problem. They can reach people socially, but cannot efficiently maintain a catalogue, collect trusted digital payments, know which orders are profitable, or create reliable delivery expectations.

The primary job is therefore not "find me a product." It is: **help me buy a known item from a known local seller and receive it when promised.**

## The wedge

The first market is a dense, bounded community: UMaT and selected Tarkwa pickup areas. Density makes each part of the model easier: buyers and sellers share practical locations; fixed pickup points reduce failed deliveries; a small team can verify sellers and resolve issues; orders cluster into economical runner routes; and word-of-mouth lowers acquisition cost.

SmartShop launches with 20–40 curated, reliable merchants in repeat-purchase or urgent categories: food/essentials, printing and stationery, accessories, thrift, hostel needs and textbooks. It does not initially list every category, accept unverified high-value used electronics, or promise nationwide delivery.

## Product promise

"Order from a verified UMaT seller. Pick up from a known point or receive it on campus within the stated time. If it fails, SmartShop resolves it."

The promise requires four primitives:

1. **Verified supply:** seller identity, reachable contact details, stock standards and category rules.
2. **Explicit fulfilment:** a pickup code or campus zone, stated fee and stated time—not a vague 'delivery available'.
3. **Buyer protection:** paid-order record, delivery proof, defined dispute window and accountable human resolution.
4. **Repeat loop:** order history, saved sellers, useful availability alerts and painless repurchase.

AI can help sellers create honest listings or make search easier. It is not the reason a buyer adopts the product and must never make pricing, authenticity or delivery promises it cannot support.

## Business model and unit economics

The early model has three transparent revenue streams: a marketplace service fee on eligible GMV, buyer-paid delivery, and later promoted listings or paid seller tools after merchants earn measurable incremental sales.

Subscriptions are deferred as a primary growth lever. A merchant should pay only after SmartShop has made them money; charging GHS 49 before that reduces quality supply.

Every order records subtotal, platform fee, delivery fee and seller payout separately. Weekly contribution margin is:

`platform fee + delivery revenue - payment processing - runner cost - refund/support cost - promotion subsidy`.

No delivery zone expands until contribution margin is positive for four consecutive weeks.

## Success criteria: 90-day pilot

Measure fulfilled orders, not sign-ups or listings. The pilot advances only when it reaches:

- 100 weekly fulfilled orders;
- 25%+ 30-day second-purchase rate among first-time buyers;
- 90%+ on-time pickup/delivery against the stated promise;
- below 3% buyer dispute rate;
- positive contribution margin after payment, delivery, refunds and support.

The activation event is a buyer completing a first fulfilled order. The retention event is a second fulfilled order within 30 days.

## Operating system

Every paid campus-delivery order receives a delivery task with a zone, promised time, runner assignment and hand-off timestamps. The operations queue is reviewed at least twice daily. Sellers who miss ready-for-pickup commitments are paused; runners who fail proof or time standards are removed. A named operator owns disputes and responds inside a published SLA.

Cash-on-delivery is a controlled experiment, not a default. It can improve trust, but creates cancellation, reconciliation and runner-cash risk. Start with prepaid MoMo/card and allow COD only for trusted sellers, low-value baskets and selected pickup points after evidence supports it.

## What SmartShop must not do yet

- Hold customer funds, promise escrow, or create a wallet without appropriate legal, payment-provider and reconciliation controls.
- Claim automated Ghana Card or facial matching while verification is manual.
- Store verification data longer than documented need or expose it to broad admin access.
- Market itself as Ghana-wide before a small local operation repeatedly meets its promise.

## Expansion logic

The next community is selected by density and repeat demand, not population size: another university, hostel cluster or commercial district with a local operator and pickup partner. SmartShop remains a local-commerce operating system while the consumer marketplace grows from proven nodes.

If SmartShop cannot create repeat fulfilled orders with positive local unit economics, pivot to the fulfilment, catalogue and payment-operations layer for existing social sellers—not a broad consumer marketplace.
